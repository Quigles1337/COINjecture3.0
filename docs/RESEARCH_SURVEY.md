# Grounding COINjecture 3.0: A Research Survey for a Derive-Don't-Trust Proof-of-Useful-Work Blockchain

## TL;DR
- **No shipped PoUW system has both eliminated the self-chosen-easy-instance problem and kept validator verification cheap on genuine NP-hard optimization work; the only provably-secure design (IOHK's Ofelimos, CRYPTO 2022) achieves it only by binding instances to the block hash via a random oracle, wrapping the answer in a SNARG, and capping "usefulness" at ≤½.** For v3, the derive-don't-trust axiom is satisfiable, but only for a narrow class of problems and most defensibly by treating useful work as an admission lottery/side-protocol rather than the fork-choice weight itself.
- **The hardest technical constraint is not proving solutions — it is generating instances that are reliably hard.** Random instances of most NP-hard problems are easy away from a narrow phase-transition band; planted-solution distributions (planted SAT/clique) and worst-case-to-average-case reductions (lattice SIS/LWE) are the only families with defensible hardness, and planting is a double-edged sword because the planter knows the answer.
- **Succinct proofs of arbitrary NP-hard solution checkers are not yet cheap enough to be the sole verification path in 2026** (zkSNARK provers run many orders of magnitude over native for nontrivial circuits), so the realistic v3 architecture is: deterministic recomputation where the checker is cheap, optimistic verification with interactive fraud proofs where it is not, and SNARKs reserved for the few places (proof aggregation, light-client sync) where they already ship. The Lean 4 spec should cover the transaction validity and state-transition function first — precisely the layer that failed v2's audit — before fork choice.

## Key Findings

1. **PoUW has a graveyard, not a track record.** Primecoin (2013) is the only long-lived deployed system, and it works precisely because its "useful" output (Cunningham/bi-twin prime chains) inherits the three properties of a hash puzzle: instances are bound to the block header hash, verification (a Fermat + Euler-Lagrange-Lifchitz test) is far cheaper than search, and difficulty is continuously adjustable via the Fermat-remainder fractional length. Protein-folding coins (Curecoin, Foldingcoin) and BOINC-style coins abandoned decentralization: they trust a central authority (Stanford's Folding@home / Pande Lab servers) to score work — exactly the "trust the miner/scorer" failure mode v2 must eliminate. Deep-learning PoUW (DLchain/PoDL, Coin.AI, WekaCoin) systematically relies on self-reported accuracy and task-publisher-supplied test sets.
2. **The academic consensus is that "usefulness" is in direct tension with the properties consensus needs.** The 2024 SoK by Andrea Merlina, Thiago Garrett, and Roman Vitenberg (University of Oslo), *"On Replacing Cryptopuzzles with Useful Computation in Blockchain Proof-of-Work Protocols"* (arXiv:2404.15735v4, submitted to ACM Computing Surveys), formalizes the block-proposal properties that cryptopuzzles satisfy — it is "the first work to present a comprehensive list of BPPs and exploited cryptopuzzle properties" — and concludes no proposed useful-work task satisfies all of them; usefulness conflicts with non-amortizability, instance-non-choosability, and cheap verification.
3. **Ofelimos is the reference design to study and to critique.** It solves instance-choice with a pre-hash target T1 calibrated to the worst-case cost of one exploration step, decouples block success from solution quality with a post-hash, and replaces validator recomputation with a SNARG covering just two of the many search steps. Its own authors cap "engine usefulness" at ½ and admit no problem class has been shown to give provably high usefulness.
4. **Instance hardness is a distributional problem, not a worst-case one.** Levin's average-case theory and decades of phase-transition work show that NP-hardness (a worst-case notion) does not imply typical-case hardness. Reliably hard, efficiently sampleable distributions are rare and mostly come from cryptographic lattices (Ajtai's worst-case-to-average-case reduction for SIS) and planted models.
5. **Verification asymmetry varies enormously by problem class.** Decision/search problems with a checkable certificate (SAT witness, a TSP tour under a threshold) verify in near-linear time from (instance, solution) alone with no metadata. Optimization ("is this the *best* tour?") has no cheap certificate of optimality — which is why every serious design converts optimization into thresholded decision ("a tour of cost ≤ B") or best-of-epoch tournaments.
6. **Succinct proving is production-real for narrow, fixed circuits and still very expensive for general computation.** Groth16/PLONK verify in milliseconds with hundreds-of-bytes-to-kB proofs, but proving cost scales quasi-linearly in circuit size with large constants; zkML proving overheads over an already-arithmetized baseline are typical (Artemis reports 3.3×–17.1× for KZG and 3.2×–17.8× for IPA with Poseidon commitments), and GPT-2-scale proving exhausted the largest AWS instance available (memory requirements beyond 4,096 GB). Filecoin ships SNARK-compressed proofs of storage at scale, and optimistic rollups (Arbitrum/Optimism) ship interactive fraud proofs — both are directly transplantable patterns.
7. **Formal verification of consensus is mature enough to anchor a spec-first workflow, and Lean 4 is a defensible choice in 2026.** CometBFT/Tendermint ship TLA+ specs (model-checked with Apalache for n≤6) alongside a Rust implementation; IronFleet, Velisarios (Coq), and Verdi established code-level verified consensus; Lean 4 blockchain/ZK verification is nascent but active (Ethereum Foundation's ArkLib/verified-zkEVM, Nethermind's Clear/Aegis for smart contracts). A single-language Rust core minimizes the audited surface; every documented Rust-in-production memory-safety failure at an FFI boundary argues for keeping the consensus-critical path free of C/C++ FFI.

## Details

### Area 1 — Prior art in Proof-of-Useful-Work chains

**Primecoin (Sunny King, 2013)** is the survival case and the template. Miners search for prime chains (Cunningham chains of the first/second kind and bi-twin chains). Three design choices make it work as a *proof of work* and not just a proof of a self-chosen easy instance:
- **Instance binding:** the origin of the prime chain must be divisible by the block-header hash, so a chain is only valid for one specific block and cannot be precomputed or reused. This is the ancestor of the derive-don't-trust axiom.
- **Cheap verification:** validators run a Fermat base-2 test plus the Euler-Lagrange-Lifchitz test for *probable* primality — deliberately not a full primality proof, because the numbers are too large. Verification is orders of magnitude cheaper than search.
- **Continuous difficulty:** King explicitly rejected prime *size* as the difficulty metric (non-linear, hurts verification) and instead used the Fermat-test remainder of the first non-prime to define a fractional chain length, giving a smooth adjustable target (~7.1 at the time of the Bitcoin Magazine writeup). Primecoin's "usefulness," however, is contested: the primes have limited scientific value, and the ACM survey classifies its usefulness contribution as marginal.

**Gapcoin** (prime gaps) is a close variant with the same number-theoretic hardness and the same limited real-world utility.

**Curecoin (2014) and Foldingcoin** are the cautionary tale directly relevant to v2's failure. Both piggyback on Stanford's Folding@home. FoldingCoin's own whitepaper admits it runs PHP on a commercial host that reads Folding@home's reported stats once a day and mints tokens proportionally; a widely-cited Hacker News critique notes it "had to completely give up decentralization" because Folding@home "could alter the distribution of coins at will." Curecoin is a PoW/PoS hybrid where folding is scored by the Pande Lab — a trusted third party. This is precisely the "self-reported / trusted-scorer" class of vulnerability v3 must make unrepresentable.

**Coinami** used a hierarchical authority model for bioinformatics (DNA read alignment): a central authority issues signed problems and verifies solutions. Useful, but authority-dependent — again violates derive-don't-trust.

**DLchain / PoDL (Proof of Deep Learning) and successors (Coin.AI, WekaCoin).** The canonical PoDL design (the 1902.03912 line) has miners train models, then full nodes pick the block whose model has the highest accuracy on a test set. Crucially, the original scheme has miners **self-report their model accuracy** and validators check from the highest claimed downward; test sets are chosen and released by task publishers. The arXiv SoK (2404.15735) rates DLchain's short-term targets as "unilaterally decided by the supplier" and its accuracy-improvement process as a random trial-and-error that undermines the bounded-variability property consensus needs. The IOHK D-PoDL paper (LatinCrypt '21) was the first to give *formal* chain-growth/quality/common-prefix proofs for DL-based PoUW via a "hash-training-hash" structure and model-referencing — a step toward derive-don't-trust, but still bound to a supplied dataset.

**Ofelimos (Matthias Fitzi, Aggelos Kiayias, Giorgos Panagiotakos, Alexander Russell — CRYPTO 2022, LNCS vol. 13508, pp. 339–369, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-15979-4_12; IACR ePrint 2021/1379)**, subtitled *"A Provably Secure Blockchain Protocol,"* is the first *provably secure* PoUW longest-chain protocol and the single most important prior-art for v3. See Area 5 for its consensus structure; its instance-binding and verification mechanisms are detailed in Areas 2–4. A genuine 2025 follow-up, *"Efficient and Proof-of-Useful-Work Friendly Local-Search for Distributed Consensus"* (Kiayias, Michel [Univ. Connecticut], Panagiotakos, Russell — IACR ePrint 2025/2091), introduces Frequently Rerandomized Local Search (FRLS) to fix the wastefulness of Doubly Parallel Local Search on steep regions of the solution space, noting that Ofelimos's "usefulness to wastefulness ratio… hinges on the properties of its underlying generic distributed local-search algorithm—Doubly Parallel Local Search (DPLS)." (Note: a paper titled "Proof-of-Useful-Work is Practical: Consensus via a Competitive Optimization Engine" is referenced in IOHK's 2025 research report but could not be independently located; treat that title as unverified.)

**Chia (Proof of Space and Time)** is the useful-adjacent comparison. It is not useful work; it is a resource proof (disk space) combined with a VDF (Wesolowski-style repeated squaring) for time. Its relevance to v3 is architectural: it shows a deployed system where the "work" is a verifiable-resource proof plus a sequential VDF, and the fork choice is on a deterministically-weighted quantity — a model where the consensus-critical quantity is derivable, not self-reported.

**Theory line (Ball–Rosen–Sabin–Vasudevan, STOC 2017 / CRYPTO 2018).** "Proofs of Useful Work" and "Proofs of Work from Worst-Case Assumptions" build PoWs whose hardness reduces from fine-grained worst-case conjectures (Orthogonal Vectors, 3SUM, All-Pairs Shortest Paths) and, critically, prove **non-amortizability** (you can't produce many proofs faster than producing each). This is the rigorous answer to "self-chosen easy instance" for a specific algebraic problem family, and the 2025 "Proofs of Useful Work from Arbitrary Matrix Multiplication" (ePrint 2025/685) extends it. The catch: the "useful" outputs (low-degree polynomial evaluations, matrix products) are useful only in a narrow sense.

**The category critique.** The Merlina–Garrett–Vitenberg SoK is the strongest statement: cryptopuzzles satisfy a set of block-proposal properties — including that instances are not miner-choosable, that solving-time variability is bounded, that verification is cheap, and that proofs are non-amortizable — and "usefulness is in direct conflict with many important properties." The arXiv 2209.03865 "Challenges of PoUW" and the emergentmind synthesis reach the same conclusion: "only tasks with publicly parameterizable, reliably hard per-instance cost (often mathematical) support full Bitcoin-style security."

### Area 2 — Canonical instance generation and verifiable sampling

The core defense against instance-shopping is to make the instance a deterministic function of committed on-chain randomness that no single party controls.

**Randomness sources, in increasing robustness:**
- **Commit-reveal** (n parties commit r_i, then reveal, output ⊕r_i): simple but suffers the last-revealer withholding attack — a malicious party sees others' reveals and aborts to bias the output.
- **VDF-hardened beacons (Unicorn, Lenstra–Wesolowski):** parties post r_i directly and the seed is H(r_1..r_n) passed through a VDF whose delay exceeds the submission window, so the last party cannot compute the impact of its choice in time. Wesolowski (EUROCRYPT 2019) and Pietrzak (ITCS 2019) give efficient VDFs from repeated squaring in groups of unknown order — evaluation is inherently sequential, verification is fast, output is unique. Chia deploys this.
- **VRF-based leader/instance selection** (Algorand-style) and **drand** (threshold BLS beacon) give bias-resistance under an honest majority/threshold.

For v3, the defensible pattern is: **derive the instance seed from a VDF-hardened beacon or from the previous block's hash passed through a VDF**, so the instance for block N is fixed before any miner can grind it, exactly as Ofelimos binds the exploration seed to H(block ‖ state).

**The deeper problem: random ≠ hard.** This is the single most important finding for problem-class selection. Worst-case NP-hardness says nothing about typical instances:
- **Random k-SAT** is easy for clause/variable ratio α far from the satisfiability threshold; the statistical-physics (cavity/replica) estimate places the SAT→UNSAT transition for random 3-SAT at critical clause density α_s ≈ 4.267 (SATLIB locates it near 4.26·n for large n). Hard instances cluster in a narrow band around this transition, so sampling "hard" random 3-SAT means sampling *at* the threshold, and even then hardness is empirical, not proven.
- **Random 2-SAT, random Hamiltonian cycle, random 2+p-SAT (p<0.4)** are provably or empirically easy despite phase transitions.
- **Random TSP, random knapsack** are typically easy; knapsack is solvable in pseudopolynomial time and random instances rarely hit hard regimes.
- Vertex-cover and SAT, though inter-reducible, have *different* random ensembles with different easy-hard structure — the reduction does not preserve typical hardness.

**Planted instances** are the main tool to get a hard instance *with a known answer* (enabling cheap verification):
- **Planted SAT:** fix an assignment, sample clauses satisfied by it. Verification is trivial (check the assignment), but the planter knows the solution — unacceptable if the miner plants. It only helps if the *protocol* plants (via the beacon) and the miner must *recover* the planting.
- **Planted clique:** the Planted Clique Conjecture holds that distinguishing G(n,½) from G(n,½)+a planted clique of size k = o(√n) is hard for polynomial-time algorithms. But the "naïve security is weak" caveat is explicit: for small k a brute-force over ~2 log₂ n-node subsets is quasi-polynomial and often practical, so parameters must be chosen carefully.
- **Lattice problems (SVP/CVP, SIS/LWE):** the gold standard. Ajtai's worst-case-to-average-case reduction proves that solving *random* SIS instances is as hard as approximating SVP/SIVP in the *worst case* — the only family where random-instance hardness is provably reducible to a worst-case assumption. This is why post-quantum cryptography is lattice-based. The cost: the "useful" output (a short vector) has narrow external utility, and NP-hardness of SVP holds only in regimes usually *outside* those used cryptographically.

**Design implication:** planting enables cheap verification without weakening hardness *only when the planting is done by the protocol's beacon and recovery is the hard problem* — and even then the strongest guarantees exist for lattices, not for SAT/TSP/knapsack.

### Area 3 — Verification asymmetry per problem class

The derive-don't-trust axiom requires: given (instance, solution), a validator computes the score/quality deterministically with no self-reported metadata, and cheaply relative to solving.

- **SAT / MaxSAT:** decision-SAT has a perfect certificate — the assignment, checkable in linear time. MaxSAT (maximize satisfied clauses) is optimization: a claimed assignment's *count* is deterministically computable, but "is this the maximum?" has no cheap certificate. Use thresholds ("≥ m clauses satisfied").
- **Knapsack / TSP / combinatorial optimization:** a claimed tour's cost is computable in O(n) from (graph, permutation) with zero metadata — this is the property v2 lacked. But optimality is not certifiable cheaply; systems must use a threshold ("cost ≤ B") turning it into a decision problem, or a best-of-epoch tournament where the chain simply keeps the best-scoring valid solution seen. Approximation certificates (e.g., LP-duality bounds) exist for some problems and can certify "within factor ρ of optimal."
- **Graph coloring:** verifying a claimed k-coloring is trivial (check every edge); "is χ(G)=k?" is not.
- **Lattice SVP/CVP:** verifying that a claimed vector is *in* the lattice and has norm ≤ B is cheap; verifying it is *shortest* is not — again thresholded.
- **Protein folding / molecular docking:** the score (a physics energy function) is deterministically computable from a conformation, but the "best fold" is not certifiable, and in practice these systems trust a central scorer (Curecoin). Not viable under derive-don't-trust without a canonical, on-chain-specified energy function and a tournament.
- **ML training:** the fatal case. "Accuracy" depends on a test set (who chooses it?), floating-point non-determinism breaks bit-exact recomputation, and training is not a function of the instance alone (random seeds, hardware). zkML and optimistic ML verification (TAO's tolerance-aware bisection) are emerging but immature.

**General rule for v3:** prefer problem classes where the *score is a deterministic pure function of (canonical instance, submitted solution)* and where the contest is framed as **threshold-decision or best-of-epoch tournament**, never "trust the reported optimum."

### Area 4 — Succinct proofs vs recomputation

**Honest 2025–2026 cost picture:**
- **Groth16 / PLONK (pairing-based SNARKs):** proofs are hundreds of bytes to a few kB; verification is constant and milliseconds (zk-Bench: all tools verify within ~5 ms for Groth16/PLONK). Groth16 needs a per-circuit trusted setup; PLONK a universal one. Proving is the bottleneck: quasi-linear in circuit size with heavy per-gate public-key operations. Concrete anchor: SHA-256 of a 16 kB preimage takes ~8.2 s (rapidsnark) to ~134 s (snarkjs) to prove and ~9–15 GB RAM; a 1.5-million-constraint ECDSA circuit is at the upper end of the Groth16 benchmark range.
- **STARKs:** transparent (no trusted setup), post-quantum, but larger proofs and verification that grows with input size (zk-Bench: ~22 ms at 2²⁵ constraints, an order of magnitude over Groth16).
- **Nova / folding schemes (IVC):** the most promising for repeated identical steps (a local-search walk, a training loop). Per Microsoft's Nova implementation, it "achieves the smallest verifier circuit… the circuit is constant-sized and its size is about 10,000 multiplication gates" — >10× smaller than SNARK-based IVC and >100× smaller than trusted-setup-free SNARK recursion — with sub-linear per-step compressed proving. This is a natural fit for a PoUW that is a sequence of identical exploration steps, exactly Ofelimos's structure.
- **zkML overheads:** Artemis (arXiv 2409.12055) reports commit-and-prove Poseidon overheads of 3.3×–17.1× (KZG) and 3.2×–17.8× (IPA) over an *already-arithmetized* baseline, and could not complete GPT-2 proving "because of memory requirements beyond the 4096GB on the largest AWS instance available." zkDL proves an 8-layer, 10M-parameter training batch in <1 s/batch on GPU — impressive but for a small model.

**Verdict:** proving *arbitrary* NP-hard solution checkers succinctly is **not** practical in 2026 as the sole path. It is practical (and shipped) for: (a) narrow fixed circuits (hash preimages, signatures, Merkle proofs — Filecoin's PoRep/PoSt compress storage proofs into SNARKs at network scale), (b) repeated-step computations via folding, and (c) proof aggregation and light-client sync.

**The realistic alternative — optimistic verification with fraud proofs.** Arbitrum/Optimism resolve disputes by an interactive bisection game: a claimant asserts an N-step result, a challenger bisects until they disagree on a single instruction, and the L1 contract re-executes just that one step ("one-step proof"). This makes *worst-case* verification cheap for the referee while normal operation requires no proof at all. L2BEAT reports real fraud-proof games take ~70–90 moves to resolve. TAO (2510.16028) applies exactly this pattern to neural-network verification with tolerance-aware operator-level bisection. For v3, an interactive dispute game over the deterministic solution-checker (or over the search trace) is the most defensible 2026 choice where the checker is too expensive to recompute in-band.

**VDFs and Proof-of-Replication** are the deployed examples of verifiable resource expenditure: Wesolowski/Pietrzak VDFs (sequential, uniquely verifiable) and Filecoin's PoRep (a slow sealing encoding that is infeasible to regenerate on demand, committed via a Multi-SNARK). Both are models for "consensus-relevant quantity is derivable/provable, never self-reported."

### Area 5 — Consensus skeleton options

Three architectures, evaluated on the size of the consensus-critical validation surface:

**(a) Longest/heaviest chain by deterministic work score.** Fork choice weights each block by a deterministically-derivable work score. This is Primecoin and Ofelimos. Ofelimos's mechanism is the reference:
- **Two block types:** hard-to-find *ranking blocks* (Nakamoto function, fork-choice-relevant) and easy-to-find *input blocks* (function like transactions carrying local-search results so state updates propagate fast). A 2-for-1 mining scheme.
- **Three targets:** T1 (pre-hash, anti-grinding — set so grinding for an easy instance costs at least a worst-case exploration step of the DPLS algorithm), T3 (block-success threshold), T2 (the adjustable consensus-difficulty knob distinguishing ranking from input blocks). Difficulty adjustment adapts T2. The exploration seed is bound to H(block ‖ state) via the random oracle, so a miner cannot choose its instance.
- **Verification:** validators do not re-run the search; the miner attaches a SNARG proving correct computation of just two of the many exploration-step invocations (the one yielding the block-eligibility witness sT, and the best solution sbest), so verification cost is independent of the exploration-step complexity.
- **Security:** tolerates any minority (<½) of compute under the standard assumption that the adversary has no speed advantage on the exploration step; degrades gracefully to <⅓ even if the adversary can compute the step for free, because pre-hashing against T1 still costs them.
- **Known limits:** "engine usefulness" ≤ ½ (miners spend ~half their time on non-useful pre-hashing + SNARG generation), usefulness → 0 for ill-behaved exploration steps, and the authors explicitly flag that no problem class has been shown to give provably high usefulness. DPLS is wasteful on steep solution-space regions (fixed by FRLS in ePrint 2025/2091). The construction "collapses" to pure PoW when the block-type probabilities are set to p2:=1, p3:=0.

**High-variance fork-choice hazard:** if the work score has high variance (as optimization scores do), fork choice becomes manipulable — a miner sitting on a lucky high-score solution can privately build advantage, and score variance widens the private-mining/selfish-mining attack surface. This is why Ofelimos deliberately **decouples block success (a hash threshold) from solution quality (the score)** — the fork-choice-relevant quantity is a low-variance hash race, not the high-variance optimization score. v3 must copy this decoupling.

**(b) Useful work as an admission lottery into a BFT/committee finality layer.** Hybrid PoW/BFT designs — PeerCensus, ByzCoin, Hybrid Consensus (Pass–Shi), Thunderella, and PoS finality gadgets (Casper FFG, GRANDPA) — use the work only to *select committee membership or a leader*, then run BFT for finality. This shrinks the consensus-critical surface: the work only needs to be a fair, non-grindable lottery ticket; the safety of the ledger rests on the well-studied BFT layer, not on the work score. Costs: committee designs need a PKI, are not fully dynamically available, and are vulnerable to adaptive corruption of the elected committee. Thunderella's optimistic fast path (¾ honest + honest leader) falls back to the slow chain otherwise.

**(c) Useful work as a separate marketplace / side-protocol; chain secured conventionally ("decouple security from usefulness").** Filecoin is the deployed exemplar: leader election (Expected Consensus) is separated from the storage-proof marketplace, though WinningPoSt does tie block-production eligibility to holding a replica. Golem/Flux-style compute markets go further — the useful work is a paid service and the chain that settles payments uses ordinary consensus. This **minimizes the consensus-critical validation surface most aggressively**: the chain never has to validate the useful work for *safety*; it validates it only for *payment*, and can use optimistic/fraud-proof settlement there.

**Assessment for v3:** architecture (a) maximizes philosophical purity ("mining is useful") but keeps the high-variance, hardest-to-audit quantity on the consensus-critical path. Architecture (c) minimizes audited surface but arguably isn't "PoUW" in the purist sense. Architecture (b) is the pragmatic middle: useful work as a non-grindable admission lottery, BFT for finality — the useful-work verification can fail safe without forking the ledger.

### Area 6 — Formal verification of consensus

**State of the art:**
- **TLA+ / model checking:** CometBFT (successor to Tendermint) ships TLA+ specs of its consensus (including a fork-accountability spec model-checked with Apalache for n=4..6, proving agreement for f<n/3 and fork-accountability for f≥n/3). Informal Systems' Quint (an executable TLA-based language) has a Tendermint spec. This is the most battle-tested spec-first blockchain workflow, and notably it pairs a spec with a *Rust* implementation (tendermint-rs).
- **Coq:** Velisarios (BFT protocols in Coq), Verdi (Raft), and the broader verified-distributed-systems line. Ethereum's beacon-chain has had executable-spec and formal efforts (Runtime Verification's K-based work).
- **Dafny/TLA hybrid:** IronFleet (Microsoft Research) verified a Paxos-based replicated state machine end-to-end (protocol + implementation) — the canonical demonstration that a spec and an implementation can be kept in lock-step conformance.
- **Lean 4:** newer and thinner for consensus specifically, but active. The Ethereum Foundation's ArkLib / verified-zkEVM formalize proof-system security (FRI, WHIR) and ZK circuits (an embedded Lean 4 DSL for R1CS/PLONK). Nethermind's Clear and Jakob von Raumer's Aegis verify smart contracts (Starknet) in Lean. Aeneas translates Rust to Lean for functional verification. A public tutorial series (Takahashi) formally verifies simple consensus (à la Roughgarden, building toward Tendermint) in Lean 4. Lean 4 + Mathlib is a mature *mathematical* foundation; its distributed-systems tooling is emerging.

**Proven conformance patterns (what keeps implementation ≈ spec):**
1. **Code extraction** (Coq→OCaml, as in CompCert/Velisarios): the running code *is* the verified artifact. Highest assurance, but forces the implementation language.
2. **Refinement / executable specs** (IronFleet, TLA+ with an executable subset): prove the implementation refines the abstract spec.
3. **Model-based conformance testing** (CometBFT's approach): generate test traces from the TLA+ model and check the Rust implementation against them. Lower assurance than extraction but language-agnostic and practical — the pragmatic choice for a Rust core with a Lean spec, since Lean→Rust extraction is not production-ready (Aeneas goes Rust→Lean, the reverse direction).

**What to specify first — the empirical answer:** successful projects specify **the state-transition function and transaction/block validity before fork choice and networking.** This maps *exactly* onto v2's root cause: the transaction apply path (nonces, balance arithmetic, signature-to-address binding) is the highest-value, most-tractable target for a Lean 4 spec, and it is deterministic (no distributed-systems reasoning needed). Fork choice and the useful-work scoring come second; asynchronous network reasoning (the hardest to verify) last.

### Area 7 — Language and trusted-computing-base considerations

- **Single-language vs polyglot cores:** the audit-surface argument favors a single memory-safe language for the consensus-critical path. Every documented Rust-production memory-safety failure of note occurs at an **FFI boundary**: the C-Rust FFI (UCSD HotOS, "Rewrite it in Rust Considered Harmful?") shows callers can pass invalid inputs that break Rust's guarantees; stack unwinding or a panic across the FFI boundary is undefined behavior; type/ownership mismatches cause use-after-free and double-free. Real CVEs cited: rusqlite→libsqlite3 (CVE-2021-45713), xcb→libxcb (RUSTSEC-2020-0097). Cross-language bugs can even bypass CFI hardening.
- **Rust's position:** Rust is the de-facto default for new consensus implementations (tendermint-rs, Polkadot/Substrate, Solana, many others) because safe Rust eliminates the memory-safety bug class that dominates C/C++ chains. The residual risk is concentrated in `unsafe` blocks and FFI — which should be *minimized and audited* and kept off the consensus-critical path.
- **Implication for v3:** a single-language Rust consensus core with **no C/C++ FFI on the transaction-apply and fork-choice paths**, `unsafe` confined to audited, spec-covered modules, and any heavy native solver (SAT/TSP/lattice) running as an *untrusted external process* whose output is validated by the safe-Rust checker — never linked into the consensus binary via FFI. This structurally prevents the FFI vulnerability class and keeps the audited surface small.

## Recommendations

**Staged design decisions, with the benchmarks that would change them.**

**Stage 1 — Fix the derive-don't-trust foundation (do first, independent of problem choice).**
1. Specify the state-transition function and transaction-validity predicate in Lean 4 *first*: nonce monotonicity, balance-conservation arithmetic (no overflow/underflow), and signature-to-address binding. Derive or conformance-test the Rust implementation against this spec via model-based testing (CometBFT pattern). This directly closes v2's audited root cause and is the highest-ROI verification work.
2. Make every consensus-relevant quantity a deterministic pure function of committed on-chain data: the problem instance is derived from a VDF-hardened beacon seed (Unicorn/Wesolowski) bound to the parent block hash; the work score is recomputed by validators from (canonical instance, submitted solution); solve time and difficulty are never read from miner-supplied fields.

**Stage 2 — Choose the problem class under the axiom.** Adopt a *threshold-decision or best-of-epoch tournament* framing, never "reported optimum." Ranked viability:
- **Most defensible:** lattice short-vector / SIS-style problems (provable worst-case-to-average-case hardness, cheap membership+norm check) or the Ball et al. fine-grained family (OV/3SUM/APSP, proven non-amortizable) if "usefulness" can tolerate their narrow outputs.
- **Defensible with care:** planted-SAT / planted-clique where the *protocol* plants via the beacon and *recovery* is the work — with parameters chosen well above brute-force regimes.
- **Avoid as consensus-critical:** ML training (non-determinism, test-set trust), protein folding/docking (trusted scorer), and raw TSP/knapsack at random (typically easy). These can live in a marketplace side-protocol (Stage 3c) but must not weight fork choice.

**Stage 3 — Choose the consensus skeleton to minimize audited surface.**
- **Recommended:** architecture (b) — useful work as a **non-grindable admission lottery** into a BFT/committee finality layer, *plus* Ofelimos-style **decoupling of block success (a low-variance hash threshold) from solution quality (the high-variance score)**. This keeps the high-variance optimization score off the safety-critical path and lets useful-work verification fail safe.
- If purist single-chain PoUW is required, adopt architecture (a) with the full Ofelimos T1/T2/T3 + SNARG structure and FRLS-style search to bound waste — accepting the ≤½ usefulness ceiling.
- If maximum auditability is the priority, architecture (c): secure the chain conventionally and run useful work as a fraud-proof-settled compute market (Filecoin/Golem model).

**Stage 4 — Verification technology, staged by cost.**
- Default to **deterministic in-band recomputation** wherever the solution-checker is near-linear (SAT witness, tour cost, coloring, lattice-vector norm). This is the cheapest and most auditable.
- Where the checker is too expensive, use **optimistic verification with interactive fraud proofs** (Arbitrum bisection / TAO operator-level bisection). This is the realistic 2026 default for expensive checkers.
- Reserve **SNARKs** for what already ships: proof aggregation, light-client sync, and repeated-identical-step work via **Nova/folding** (a natural fit for a local-search walk). **Benchmark threshold that would promote SNARKs to the main path:** when proving the solution-checker circuit costs <~10× the native solve time and verifies in <100 ms at target instance sizes — not the case today for arbitrary NP-hard checkers.

**Stage 5 — TCB discipline.** Single-language Rust consensus core; no C/C++ FFI on transaction-apply or fork-choice paths; heavy native solvers run as untrusted external processes validated by safe-Rust checkers; `unsafe` confined to audited, spec-covered modules.

## Caveats and honest disagreements in the literature
- **Whether "genuinely useful" and "consensus-secure" can coexist at all is unsettled.** The Merlina–Garrett–Vitenberg SoK and the "Challenges of PoUW" paper argue usefulness structurally conflicts with the properties consensus needs; Ofelimos proves you can get *provable security* but concedes usefulness ≤½ and that no problem class yet gives provably high usefulness. A purist "mining is fully useful" v3 may be unachievable; a hybrid where useful work is a byproduct or a side-market is the honest fallback.
- **Instance hardness is the deepest open risk.** Outside lattices, there is no NP-hard problem with a *proven* efficiently-sampleable hard distribution. Phase-transition sampling gives *empirically* hard SAT but not proofs; planted models give known answers (dangerous if the miner plants) and can have practical brute-force attacks at small parameters. If v3's security rests on typical-case hardness of a non-lattice problem, that is an assumption, not a theorem — state it explicitly.
- **SNARK readiness is contested and moving fast.** Costs quoted here (zk-Bench, Artemis, zkDL, the Groth16 sensor benchmark) are 2024–2026 snapshots; folding schemes are improving rapidly. A claim that SNARKs are "not ready" is true for arbitrary NP checkers today but may flip for specific structured checkers within the design horizon — re-benchmark before finalizing.
- **Fork-choice variance vs usefulness is a genuine tension.** Low-variance fork choice (hash threshold) is what makes Ofelimos secure, but it also means the *fork-choice-relevant* work is not the useful work — the useful work rides in input blocks. This is a philosophical compromise, not a solved problem.
- **Lean 4 for consensus is promising but immature.** Its mathematical core and ZK-circuit tooling are strong; distributed-systems/consensus libraries are nascent versus Coq (Velisarios) and TLA+ (CometBFT). The lowest-risk path is Lean 4 for the deterministic state machine + model-based conformance testing of the Rust code, not full end-to-end extraction (which Lean does not yet support in the Rust direction).
- **Source-quality flags:** several PoUW "usefulness" claims come from project self-promotion (IOHK blog, Curecoin/Foldingcoin sites, vendor benchmark repos) and should be read as advocacy; the peer-reviewed SoKs and IACR eprints are the more reliable anchors. The exact follow-up Ofelimos paper title in the task could not be verified and may be a conflation with ePrint 2025/2091 (FRLS).
